# RCCG Tabernacle of Praise — Identity and Media Skill

This skill gives the **church-specific context and creative requirements**, not a replacement for general logo-design expertise.

## Required reading
1. [Church profile](references/church-profile.md) — established parish context, communication audience, programs, and unknowns.
2. [Brand brief](references/brand-brief.md) — approved decisions versus rejected/experimental design ideas.
3. [Production standards](references/production-standards.md) — logo, typography, flyers, photos, legal rights, accuracy and sign-off.
4. Also read repository `AGENTS.md` and any newer approved brand guidelines. **New explicit user decisions supersede exploratory history.** Surface conflicts before proceeding.

## Identity architecture

- **Standalone parish wordmark:** `Tabernacle of Praise`.
- **Descriptive church name:** `RCCG Tabernacle of Praise`.
- **Denominational parent:** the Redeemed Christian Church of God (RCCG), based in context in Istanbul, Türkiye.
- Develop a **distinctive independent parish symbol and wordmark**. For combined official lockups, display the official RCCG emblem **separately** and unmodified. Do not AI-generate or trace it. Only use an authorized source asset or an explicitly marked internal placeholder.
- Aim for **modern, minimal, expressive, elegant, warm, welcoming** branding — avoid both clutter and generic sterile iconography.

## Current status: not an approved logo

An early architectural/tabernacle direction was liked. Subsequent ultra-simple Open Tabernacle, Lifted Canopy, Gathered Arch, and arch-with-stick-figure variations did not meet the user's expectations; **do not recycle them through slight changes**. User-shared church identity images show the desired caliber of symbolic integration, negative space, line quality, and intentional typography: **not shapes to copy**.

Harp/string/musical rhythm may communicate *Praise* but remain **exploratory**, not required. Compare an equally strong non-harp solution. Avoid ending up with an exclusive choir/worship-band identity. Exact logo, fonts and HEX palette await approval.

## Workflow for logos and identity

1. State the task and distinguish confirmed, exploratory, rejected and missing decisions. Surface important unresolved questions before significant phase changes.
2. Study the user's supplied imagery analytically. Extract design principles; **do not trace, mimic or create look-alikes**.
3. If available, use the separate `logo-design` skill for concept methodology, SVG construction, optical checks, originality assessment and deliverables.
4. Present distinct concepts at equal scale and in monochrome first; then compare tasteful color/typography. Make symbol and wordmark feel intentionally related.
5. Validate at real phone/avatar sizes, monochrome/reverse, signage, and official two-logo configurations.
6. Provide editable SVGs/source and a clear review presentation. Discuss risks and limitations. Mark exploratory materials `DRAFT — NOT FOR PUBLICATION`; pause for approval before finalizing.

## Workflow for church media

Verify event theme, date/time, venue, Scripture, speaker, photos/rights, and call-to-action. Use approved brand components; create varied layouts but consistent recognition. Prioritize authentic authorized parish photos; conceptual licensed imagery must never be presented as a photograph of an RCCG TOP service. Avoid color overload, unreadable text, crowded effects, fabricated facts, and publishing without human approval.

## Boundaries and voice

Never invent official mission/vision, doctrine, staff, schedules, locations, service claims, phone/email/social handles, or quotations. No automatic publishing or approval. Keep church communications respectful, natural, hopeful, welcoming and clear; avoid generic hype. See supporting references for finer-grained constraints.
