# Shared Rhythm — draft development

**DRAFT — NOT FOR PUBLICATION.** The user favored Shared Rhythm in the top-right of [Praise Study 07](../../praise-study-07/exports/praise-comparison.png). This development preserves that recognizable drawing rather than substituting another concept.

Four unequal upright folded planes turn around an open forecourt. The higher inner-right face gives an upward accent; the lower outside faces maintain an inviting opening. The architectural gathering suggests *Tabernacle*, while their changing heights and intervals suggest collective *Praise* through rhythm. Praise remains abstract; these are not literal strings or musical notation.

- **v1:** faithful new vector reconstruction of the favored generated raster.
- **v2 / primary:** widens the first interval by two units. It preserves the favored silhouette, projection, open court and asymmetric rhythm.
- **v3:** shortens the second plane's projecting apex and raises the two inner lower edges by three units. Retained as an optical alternative; the conservative v2 remains primary for this review.

The four faces deliberately remain separate. Their slight tapers communicate turning planes; automatically snapping every edge vertically would change that feel. All paths are flat filled outlines without live text, embedded rasters, strokes, masks or effects. Black and white reverse use identical geometry. No final reverse compensation, minimum size or construction standard has been approved.

Actual PNGs were rendered and inspected at 512, 64, 32, 24 and 16 pixels, in black and reverse. The overall gesture survives at 16 pixels, but two adjacent faces touch through antialiasing. The four planes remain separate at 24 pixels and above in these renders. **16 pixels remains a limitation requiring a separate optical cut if this direction proceeds.** The faceted spatial reading is clearer at 32 pixels and above.

The main risk is association with a cultural venue, property company or sculptural architecture. The full church name will carry much of the Christian specificity. This work does not establish legal originality or replace a trademark review. The geometry audit flags slight face tapers and unequal vertical margins; those are documented, not interpreted as aesthetic approval.

Editable files: [primary.svg](primary.svg), [white reverse](reverse.svg), [reverse on black](reverse-on-black.svg), and [builder](source/build.py). The builder requires Python, Pillow and Inkscape. Its annotation font is the repository's existing Noto Sans file; the symbol itself has no font dependency. The source raster remains unchanged in Study 07. No new typeface, palette or official RCCG lockup is proposed here.
