# Shared Rhythm cinematic motion — visual review

**DRAFT — NOT FOR PUBLICATION.** Reviewed 22 October 2026. This is a craft review of the motion study, not final identity, soundtrack or publishing approval.

The piece now has a distinct visual progression: separate curved material voices gather, the four original folded faces rise into an architectural object, and the object settles into the parish identity. The warm matte surfaces, tapered ribbon profiles, moving grazing light and change from close detail to a wider view give it more expression than the earlier small vertical-lift study. The open space between the faces remains part of the composition.

The opening was revised after inspection so a visible gesture begins by about 0.4 seconds and the gathering/fold sequence occupies the frame deliberately. The broad ribbons have tapered profiles rather than round tube ends. Secondary strands are absorbed as the upright forms resolve, leaving a quieter silhouette. These choices make the movement feel connected to the mark's construction instead of a particle effect added around it.

## Findings

The fold and perspective changes are visibly different from a flat logo simply sliding upward. The central gesture remains coherent through the close-to-wide transition. The final symbol reaches its intended position before the whole RCCG endorsement and full name fade in, avoiding a duplicate mark or a sudden crossfade between unrelated compositions.

The closing layout retains the larger separate RCCG seal and the complete two-line **Tabernacle of Praise** name below the parish symbol. It has 3.5 seconds of still reading time. The source renderer uses the exact supplied closing PNG from 10.5 seconds onward; sampled encoded frames at 10.6 and 13 seconds look stable and legible. H.264 compression is lossy, so decoded pixels are not claimed to be identical to the source PNG.
