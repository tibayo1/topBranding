# RCCG Tabernacle of Praise — Strategic Logo Exploration

We need to rethink the logo development process.

I am not satisfied with the latest logo explorations. The introduction of harp and string symbolism has increasingly dominated the concepts, narrowing the exploration and distracting from the complete meaning of "Tabernacle of Praise."

I want to develop an original, meaningful, sophisticated church identity that can serve the church for many years.

This is a strategic creative exploration, not another quick round of logo variations.

## 1. Begin with Research, Not Artwork

Read AGENTS.md, the rccg-top-identity skill, and the installed logo-design skill.

Research the biblical, historical, architectural, and conceptual meanings of:

- Tabernacle
- The presence of God
- Sacred dwelling and gathering
- Worship and praise
- Thanksgiving and joyful expression
- Unity and community in worship

Study relevant passages, including Exodus 25:8, Exodus 40:34–35, Psalm 100:4, Psalm 150, and Hebrews 9.

Distinguish biblical descriptions from modern artistic interpretations. Do not assume that every religious symbol accurately represents the biblical tabernacle.

Do not invent an official theological position, mission statement, or vision for the parish.

The research should help generate meaningful, original visual ideas.

## 2. Reconsider What the Logo Should Communicate

The name "Tabernacle of Praise" contains two powerful ideas:

TABERNACLE — A place associated with God's presence, sacred dwelling, meeting, and belonging.

PRAISE — Worship, thanksgiving, joyful expression, and the response of people to God.

The goal is to explore how these two meanings can be expressed through one coherent visual identity.

I do not want two unrelated icons placed together.

I want a compelling symbol whose form, structure, and underlying meaning belong together.

Ideally, someone encountering the logo can appreciate its visual quality immediately and discover a deeper connection to the church's name when the design is explained.

## 3. Start a Fresh Conceptual Exploration

Do not limit your exploration to arches, harps, crosses, or existing concepts.

Investigate at least six conceptual territories:

A. The Dwelling of Praise — Sacred dwelling and worship integrated into one symbol.

B. Enter His Courts — Access, welcome, a threshold, and entering God's presence with praise.

C. The Presence Within — Sacred space, light, and the idea of divine presence.

D. One House, Many Voices — Collective worship, unity, belonging, and gathering.

E. A Dwelling that Sings — Architectural form and the movement or rhythm of praise.

F. The Living Wordmark — A custom typographic identity where the letters or wordmark themselves express meaningful symbolism.

These are starting points, not creative limitations.

You have permission to develop additional, better conceptual directions if your research supports them.

Do not force musical instruments into the identity. A harp may be considered where it genuinely improves the concept, but it must not dominate the exploration.

## 4. Explore Broadly Before Refining

Develop at least 12 genuinely different initial visual ideas distributed across the conceptual territories.

Avoid superficial variations of one icon.

Work in monochrome initially so the strength of each form can be evaluated independently of attractive colors.

Explore different design approaches where justified:

- Symbolic fusion
- Negative-space design
- Architectural abstraction
- Custom typography and ligatures
- Geometry and proportion
- Organic and expressive forms
- Minimal yet meaningful symbolism

Do not assume that more detail or more Christian symbols make the logo better.

Choose the four strongest concepts for deeper development. Explain why you selected them and why the others were set aside.

## 5. Design Quality and Originality

Use the existing inspiration references as examples of craftsmanship, originality, symbolic integration, and visual sophistication.

Do not copy or closely imitate them.

I do not want:
- Generic house-and-cross logos.
- Simplistic arches containing stick figures.
- Clip-art harps or musical notes.
- Unrelated symbols combined into one mark.
- Geometry that resembles a random technology startup.
- Designs that require lengthy explanations to justify arbitrary shapes.
- Different concepts that are merely recolored versions of the same idea.
- Visual complexity that disappears at small sizes.

The identity should feel modern, minimal but expressive, spiritually meaningful, welcoming, enduring, and distinctive.

Do not prioritize visual trends over lasting recognition.

## 6. Typography Matters

The standalone wordmark is:

Tabernacle of Praise

Do not treat the wordmark as an afterthought.

Explore strong, appropriately licensed typefaces and, where valuable, customized letterforms.

Test the relationship between the symbol and the full name.

The logo must work in horizontal and stacked arrangements.

It must also be compatible with the unchanged official RCCG emblem placed alongside the parish identity.

Do not copy, modify, or regenerate the RCCG emblem.

## 7. Color Is Still Open

We have not selected an official palette.

Explore color only after developing strong monochrome concepts.

Do not automatically select navy and gold, green, or burgundy simply because those palettes appeared in previous studies.

Our permanent color philosophy is restrained, contemporary, and flexible enough to support different church programs.

## 8. Presentation and Evaluation

Create a high-quality browser-based concept exploration with:

- The research findings and conceptual foundations.
- A concise explanation of the six design territories.
- At least 12 distinct initial sketches or vector explorations.
- Four developed shortlisted concepts.
- Large monochrome logo views.
- Full wordmark lockups.
- Construction and negative-space explanations.
- Small-size readability tests.
- Light and dark background tests.
- Limited realistic identity applications.
- Editable SVG artwork for shortlisted concepts.

For each shortlisted concept, show the connection between the church's name and the visual form.

Clearly explain why the design choices are meaningful instead of relying on invented symbolism after drawing the logo.

Evaluate whether each concept could serve as the church's recognizable identity for many years.

## 9. Review and Approval

Do not treat any existing logo concept as approved.

Concept B and Concept C from the previous round are historical explorations, not mandatory finalists.

Do not build the weekly flyer generator yet.

Keep all artwork labeled DRAFT — NOT FOR PUBLICATION.

Store the research, sketches, editable concepts, and presentation in organized project folders and commit the review artifacts to the repository.

Start by producing a concise research-based identity strategy and your proposed conceptual territories.

Then develop the initial explorations and shortlist four strong candidates.

Pause for my review before selecting or finalizing an identity.

The objective is not speed or the number of logos produced.

The objective is to create an original, carefully considered, meaningful, professional, and lasting visual identity for Tabernacle of Praise.
