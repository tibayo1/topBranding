# A dwelling made expressive by the people who praise

**DRAFT — NOT FOR PUBLICATION. Research-based creative strategy for review; not parish doctrine, mission, or an approved identity.**

## The proposition

Develop one recognizable form in which **dwelling and response belong together**. The containing space should be made by the same gesture that opens, gathers, or lifts it. The full name should make the parish identifiable before a viewer knows the explanation. The explanation should reward attention, not rescue an arbitrary shape.

No previous logo is a finalist. B, C, and D remain historical studies. Harp symbolism is one possible expression of praise, not the organizing premise. This round is monochrome: there is no assumed palette or permanent type system.

## What the source texts support

- **Dwelling among people:** Exodus 25:8 describes a sanctuary so God may dwell “among them.” It supports a relationship between sacred dwelling and a people, rather than an identity about real estate alone.
- **Presence is not a pictogram:** Exodus 40:34–35 describes cloud covering the Tent of Meeting and glory filling the tabernacle. It does not prescribe a cloud, sun, flame, or beam as a logo for God. Light or a meaningful unfilled center would be a contemporary design interpretation.
- **Thanksgiving is a movement and response:** Psalm 100:1–5 connects gladness, singing, entering, thanksgiving, praise, and belonging. A threshold can express invitation, but the psalm is not a plan of the wilderness tabernacle.
- **Praise has many expressions:** Psalm 150 includes multiple instruments, dancing, and “everything that has breath.” An instrument is possible but insufficient to stand for the whole parish by default.
- **A sanctuary has theological context:** Hebrews 9 describes earthly sanctuary arrangements and restricted priestly access, then develops its Christological argument. It is not a mandate to draw furniture, reproduce a plan, or imply that everyone gathered in the Holy of Holies.
- **Construction offers a visual vocabulary:** Exodus 26–27 describes joined textiles, coverings, upright boards, a veil, screens, and a rectangular court. Numbers 4 describes transport. Joined material, a shared interior, layering, and movement are credible design starting points; a dome or generic Gothic arch is not established by those descriptions.

Exact wording and retrieval provenance are retained in [scriptural foundations](scriptural-foundations.md), [architecture and symbolism](architecture-and-symbolism.md), and [the selected source text](sources/scriptural-excerpts.json). Third-party public-domain text mirrors were accessible. Several scholarly sites could not be retrieved in this environment; historical claims therefore remain conservative and text-based, with no claim of archaeological verification.

## Six territories and the question each asks

| Territory | Design question | Form vocabulary | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — The Dwelling of Praise | Can the covering itself become an act of uplift? | Joined material, folded surfaces, holding and opening | A roof with a separate worship icon underneath |
| B — Enter His Courts | Can access and thanksgiving become one spatial gesture? | Open perimeter, processional space, generous threshold | Generic door/arch, maze, or an exclusive fortress |
| C — The Presence Within | Can the interior matter more than the container? | Negative space, a held opening, restrained contrast | Claiming to depict God, arbitrary rays, candle clip-art |
| D — One House, Many Voices | Can distinct contributions make one shared dwelling? | Convergence, interdependence, varied rhythm, one center | Stick figures, heads around a circle, corporate networking icon |
| E — A Dwelling that Sings | Can structural rhythm carry expression without an instrument? | Pleats, cadence, alternating solid and space | Harp strings under another name, audio equalizer, decorative sound wave |
| F — The Living Wordmark | Can the full name carry both presence and praise? | Letter relationships, open counters, shared strokes, a measured lift | Another T/P badge, illegible ligatures, arbitrary flourishes |

## How to judge the initial work

Twelve genuinely different studies will test these questions before four are refined. They need different silhouettes, spatial logic, or mark types, rather than twelve arrangements of the same frame. Selection will favor a clear name-to-form connection, immediate visual quality, a memorable silhouette, an inviting character, durable reproduction, and a credible relationship with the full wordmark.

A weak idea does not advance because it is easy to explain. A strong abstract idea still needs candid discussion of its alternate readings. All rejected initial studies remain visible with reasons so the shortlist is reviewable.

## Typography is part of the idea

Explore licensed humanist serif and open sans approaches, plus a genuinely typographic territory. Shape and space in the full phrase matter more than a clever abbreviation. Prepare horizontal and stacked arrangements, preserve “of” at a readable size, and outline review wordmarks so another machine does not substitute a font. Candidate fonts and licenses are documented separately; none becomes the church's type system by being tested here.

## Review conditions and open decisions

Compare in black/white and reversed, with actual 64/32/24/16px symbol tests and full-name lockups at practical widths. Add only limited identity applications, not a weekly flyer generator. The official RCCG emblem remains a separately labeled reservation until an authorized source asset is supplied. Its actual color competition and formal lockup compatibility cannot yet be verified.

The review asks which relationship the parish wants to lead with: sacred dwelling, welcome, presence, collective worship, structural rhythm, or the full name. It does not ask for final palette, final font, or publication approval. AGENTS.md and the RCCG identity skill remain unchanged.
